# Field water balance layers

## Field balance (the home layer)

### What it shows

One picture of the water budget of a field for one day, one year, or the twenty-year average: rain and irrigation arriving, transpiration, soil evaporation and interception leaving upward, drainage leaving downward, and the nitrogen leached with it. The soil profile is drawn to 100 cm with three depth marks A, B and C, the sensor at the chosen depth, the roots as they grow and die back, and the wetting front.

![Field balance: arrows are the fluxes (mm per year in "Average year" view), the profile shows the root zone, the sensor, and drainage below 1 m. The equation strip under the picture is the closed budget. The numbered arrows are explained in the atlas of chapter 18, under the same screen.](images/full/field_s0_p1.jpg){width=72%}

### The control bar under the drawing

| Button | Meaning |
|:--|:--|
| **Day by day** | The simulated day on the field clock; arrows show that day's fluxes ($T_f$, $I$, $T$, $E$, interception, $D$). |
| **All 20 years** | Table and bars of every simulated year (rain, irrigation, interception, evaporation, transpiration, drainage, storage change, crop, deepest roots). Click a year. |
| **Average year** | The 20-year means of Engine 1 (Eqs. 5.4–5.12). |
| **◀ year / year ▶** | Previous or next simulated year. |
| **1× 2× 4× 8×** and speed slider | Play speed. |
| **Play / Pause** | Animates the clock. |

### How each output is generated

| On the picture | Generated by |
|:--|:--|
| Precipitation, Irrigation, Transpiration, Soil evaporation, Interception, Drainage (mm) in *Average year* | Engine 1: $P$, $I$, $T$, $E$, $E_{\mathrm{other}}$, $D$ of Eqs. (5.4)–(5.12) |
| The same in *Day by day* | Engine 2 daily fluxes after scaling, Eqs. (5.15)–(5.25) |
| Equation strip "Rain + Irrigation = Transpiration + Soil evap. + Interception + Drainage + Storage" | The closure of Eq. (5.12): $P + I = T + E + E_{\mathrm{other}} + D + \Delta S$ |
| Sensor label "depletion 0.02 / irrigates at 0.38" | Engine 2 layer $j_s$: $f_{j_s} = 1 - s_{j_s}$ against $f_T$ |
| Roots (depth, density) | Eqs. (5.19)–(5.20) |
| Wetting front | Depth of the deepest layer reached by the last infiltration event |
| Topsoil pore space bar | Pore index $p$ of Eq. (8.1) for the chosen tillage and consolidation rate |
| Drainage below 1 m | $D$ (mm), the water leaving layer 40 |
| N leaching (kg ha$^{-1}$) | $N_L$ of Eq. (5.10) |
| Yield tag (top right) | $Y\cdot f_{\mathrm{crop}}$ from Eq. (5.8) |

### Step-by-step: checking a budget

1. Open *Field balance* and choose **Average year**.
2. Read the five numbers over the arrows and the strip below. Check that rain plus irrigation minus the four sinks equals the storage change in brackets.
3. Change **Rainfall scale** to $\times$1.3. The rain, drainage and N leaching arrows thicken.
4. Press **Pin results as baseline** (Ctrl+B) *before* each change to see the difference on the Dashboard tiles.
5. Click **Day by day**, Play, and watch irrigation events pause the clock (Section 4.5).

## Soil, plant and air (S1)

### What it shows

The soil–plant–atmosphere continuum as one water column "from the particles to the sky": a section of the soil with roots, the plant, and the air above; five irrigation layouts (**Furrow, Basin, Border strip, Sprinkler, Drip**) that you can tap, which set the irrigation method; and, on the right, the **water potential along the path** from the air to the deep soil. Two data sources are labelled with badges: *DAISY* (taken from your simulations) and *DERIVED* (computed by the workbench along the path).

![Soil, plant and air layer: potentials from the air to the deep soil. *Derived* badges mark the quantities computed along the path; *Daisy* badges mark simulation data. The numbered arrows are explained in the atlas of chapter 18, under the same screen.](images/full/spac_s0_p1.jpg){width=72%}

### Governing equations

**Soil.** The suction in each layer is the suction that gives that layer's depletion, from the inverse of Eq. (5.3), floored at 30 cm:

$$
h_i = \max\{30,\ h(f_i)\},\qquad f_i = 1 - s_i,\qquad
\psi_{\mathrm{soil},i} = -h_i\cdot 9.807\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{MPa\ cm^{-1}}.
\tag{7.1}
$$

*Provenance (Eq. 7.1):* **[Literature]**: inverse of Eq. (5.3); the 30 cm floor is engineered.

The *root-zone* suction is the root-density-weighted mean, $h_{RZ} = \sum_i \rho_i h_i\big/\sum_i \rho_i$, and the *sensor* and *deep-soil* values are $h_{j_s}$ and $h_{N-3}$.

**Soil conductivity.** The relative conductivity at the root zone uses the Mualem–van Genuchten form, Eq. (5.2), with $S_e = S_e(h_{RZ})$ from Eq. (5.1).

**Along the path.** The plant is a chain of series resistances, in the spirit of the catenary (Ohm's-law) description of water flow through the soil–plant–atmosphere continuum (van den Honert, 1948). With $E$ the day's transpiration (mm d$^{-1}$, from Eq. 5.25) and $R_{\mathrm{soil}}$, $R_{\mathrm{root}}$, $R_{\mathrm{xyl}}$, $R_{\mathrm{leaf}}$ resistances in MPa per (mm d$^{-1}$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{\mathrm{soil}} &= 0.03\,\max\{K_r,\,0.002\}^{-0.55}, \quad R_{\mathrm{root}} = 0.10,\quad R_{\mathrm{xyl}} = 0.05,\quad R_{\mathrm{leaf}} = 0.08 &&(7.2)\\
\psi_{\mathrm{root}} &= \psi_{RZ} - a\,E\,R_{\mathrm{soil}}, \qquad
\psi_{\mathrm{stem}} = \psi_{\mathrm{root}} - a\,E\,R_{\mathrm{root}}, &&(7.3)\\
\psi_{\mathrm{leaf}} &= \mathrm{clip}\bigl(\psi_{\mathrm{stem}} - a\,E\,(R_{\mathrm{xyl}} + R_{\mathrm{leaf}});\ -3.2,\ 0\bigr), &&(7.4)
\end{aligned}
$$

*Provenance (Eq. 7.2--7.4):* **[Engineered]**: series-resistance chain after van den Honert (1948); resistance values chosen for the workbench.

where $a=1$ when canopy cover exceeds 0.05 (a crop is present) and $a=0$ otherwise (fallow). Stomata are drawn as *closing* when $\psi_{\mathrm{leaf}} < -1.6$ MPa.

**The air.** Water potential of the air above the canopy follows the thermodynamic relation between potential and relative humidity,

$$
\psi_{\mathrm{air}} = \frac{R\,T}{V_w}\,\ln \mathrm{RH} \;\approx\; 135.5\,\ln\mathrm{RH}\ \ \mathrm{MPa}
\tag{7.5}
$$

*Provenance (Eq. 7.5):* **[Literature]**: Nobel (2009).

(Nobel, 2009), where $R$ is the gas constant, $T\approx 293$ K and $V_w = 18\times10^{-6}$ m$^3$ mol$^{-1}$ the molar volume of water. Relative humidity is $\mathrm{RH} = \mathrm{clip}(0.66 - 0.03\,\Delta T;\,0.35,\,0.90)$ with $\Delta T$ the temperature shift. At RH = 66 % the air is at $-56$ MPa, as shown.

**What is literature and what is engineered.** Eqs. (7.1), (7.5) and (5.2) are physical relations. The resistances in (7.2)–(7.4) are **[Engineered]** values chosen so that a typical transpiring cereal reaches $\psi_{\mathrm{leaf}}$ of about $-1$ to $-1.5$ MPa and so that stress develops as the soil dries; they are *not* measured resistances, and the layer labels them "derived". **Caution.** Evaluated over mid-season crop days in all ten soils (Paper 3, Section 5.1), the relative conductivity at the root-zone suction is usually below the floor of 0.002 in Eq. (7.2), so $R_{\mathrm{soil}}$ sits at its cap (0.92 MPa per mm d$^{-1}$) and the mean leaf potential is $-1.8$ to $-2.3$ MPa in every soil, below the intended range. The leaf, stem and root potentials of this layer therefore illustrate the series-resistance concept and should not be read as plant water status.

### Step-by-step

1. Open *Soil, plant and air*.
2. Tap a layout. Furrow, basin and border strip set the **Surface** method; Sprinkler sets **Overhead**; Drip sets **Drip 20 to 30 cm**.
3. Drag or tap in the soil to move the **sensor depth** (the control snaps to the 5-cm list).
4. Change the **date** to a growing-season day and compare the potentials: with a crop present the leaf falls below $-1$ MPa as the soil dries; on a fallow date the path is flat (resistance terms are zero).
5. Move the **Trigger** slider and note that the *sensor* suction tile follows the same depletion as the dashboard.

## Profile evolution (V2)

### What it shows

How the pressure head profile develops through the season in a year, side by side for **irrigated** and **rainfed** soil columns, with the sensor depth shown by a dot. In *All 20 years* mode it draws the pressure head at the sensor through every year and a heat strip of the mean suction of each growing season (darker is drier). Text states the driest and wettest rainfed season and the share of the 0–60 cm root zone beyond the trigger.

### How it is generated

* The **1994** profile is the Daisy-simulated pressure head at every depth, irrigated and rainfed (the only year with full-depth output, as the drawing says).
* In the other years only the 20 cm sensor is available; the columns are shaded only around the sensor depth.
* *Share of the root zone beyond the trigger* is

$$
\mathrm{share} = \frac{\sum_{i:\ z_i\le 60\,\mathrm{cm},\ h_i>h_T}\Delta z_i}{\sum_{i:\ z_i\le 60\,\mathrm{cm}}\Delta z_i},
\tag{7.6}
$$

*Provenance (Eq. 7.6):* **[Engineered]**: indicator defined for the workbench.

the layer-thickness-weighted fraction of the 0–60 cm profile whose suction exceeds the trigger, evaluated for the irrigated and the rainfed profile of 1994 (the year with full-depth output).
* The mean suction of a season, used for the heat strip and for the "driest season" statements, is the geometric mean $m_y=10^{\frac{1}{183}\sum_d \log_{10}h_d}$ over the growing season; the median across years $m_{\mathrm{med}}$ also drives the rainfall year factor $\gamma_y$ (Eq. 5.15).

### Step-by-step

1. Open *Profile evolution* and choose **All 20 years**.
2. Click a year in the heat strip to select it; click a column to put the sensor at that depth.
3. Move the trigger: the share beyond the trigger is recomputed.
4. Choose **1994 detail** to see the full profile through the season; press Play to step through it.
