# `\texorpdfstring{\chapicon{partgrey}{★}}{}`{=latex}What changed from version 1.0 to version 1.1

## The window

* The program, its title bar, the status bar and the About window show **1.1.0**. The build removes any older program before building, and the program clears its cache on start, so an old version cannot be shown by mistake.
* The **Layers panel** has a new order: Irrigation design, Irrigation control, Mulch and evaporation, Soil structure and tillage, Deterministic phenotyping, Field water balance, Seasonal data, Crops, Feedback loops. The short codes of the layers (S2, V1, N4, …) are gone; layers are named by their names only.
* The **year strip** and the date bar under the drawings are gone; the year (1980 to 2100) and the date are set in Properties and on the toolbar.
* The **dashboard** is laid out like the Properties panel and every block folds on its own; the Charts tab draws the hydrograph, the times of the irrigation and where the water goes for each design (Chapter 14).
* **Properties**: the *Weather of years after 1999* selector and the explanatory notes under it, under the consolidation rate and under the design shortcuts have gone; their content is in this manual. *Weather source* chooses the built-in record, an imported file or the online weather.
* The name of the simulation model is no longer shown anywhere in the program; badges that said so now say *Simulated*.
* Labels are drawn after the pictures and moved apart when they would overlap; text near the edge of a drawing is moved inwards instead of being cut; long drawings take their full height at once.
* On screens narrower than 1500 pixels the docks give way so that the drawing keeps its size; the drawing always fits the window, nothing is cut off on the right.
* The selected layer is marked in light blue; the Play/Pause button is small.
* The first block of the Results tab gives the key numbers of the open layer (Chapter 14).
* Layers that play by changing a setting (each soil in turn, rainfall from dry to wet) wait for **Play** in the program, so Properties and the dashboard keep your values.

* Variable-rate irrigation is also computed cell by cell (10 m): the depth and the date of the next irrigation vary continuously over the field, in smooth colours stretched to the range of the field; out of the crop season the vegetation seen by Sentinel-2 drives the water use, so the maps are no longer all grey.
* All the maps of the field are drawn in continuous colours with smooth interpolation by default (indices, monitoring, erosion, retention, sensors); pixels and classes remain one choice away.

* Daisy: a new tab *Learn the Daisy files* (Documentation) teaches the files of a Daisy experiment one by one (the folder, the grammar, the soil, the management, the logs, the weather, the run file, running and reading the results, Daisy's messages, templates) with a complete sample experiment. Every file carries numbered labels explained in a table; the sample was run with Daisy 7.1.14 (water balance error 0.0 mm), its logs are included, and every error message shown was produced by Daisy itself (chapter [Learn the Daisy files](#daisy-learn)).
* Daisy: the process map keeps the original Fig. 1 as its default and adds a standalone figure with every component of Daisy: Fig. 1 unchanged, and under it every other component as a box in the same style, grouped by module, with ten new ones from the reference manual. Either figure saves as SVG or PNG (300 or 600 dpi).
* Daisy: Log plots makes one independent figure for every quantity of a log, with its own controls (kind of line, zoom by dragging, values under the cursor, titles, saving), a caption and its key numbers.
* Daisy: Run comparison gives a verdict in plain words, a table with the means, the differences, the RMS difference, the largest difference and its date, r and the Nash-Sutcliffe efficiency, three figures for every quantity, and a double-blind comparison with a sealed key.
* New SWAFL layers from Daisy runs: D2 Daisy water balance, D3 Daisy nitrogen balance and D4 Yield risk and limiting factor.


## Figures of experiments and the Daisy layers

- **To the SWAFL layers is clearer.** A status panel at the top says whether you have runs, opens the best layer for your question with one button, and every layer card is marked *ready* (green) or says in one line what it still *needs* (amber), with a button that makes those runs. The topic list and the list of all layers are folded at the bottom.
- **The data of the four papers.** The per-experiment outputs of all 10 190 Daisy runs (folder `Data/Comprehensive_Summary` of the repository) are the data of the four papers of the project; the fourth gives a closed form for the reach of a suction trigger, which the Trigger law layer shows for every soil.

* The **file viewer and editor** (Notepad++ colours) colours and numbers only the lines in view, so logs of several megabytes open, scroll and are edited at once without the program hanging; wrap is kept for files below 300 kB.
* The Layers panel and the panel of the real field are shown in this manual as they appear in version 1.1.0.

* **Why a run gave no results**: the root cause of every Daisy error in plain words (unknown name, numerical problem, value out of range, missing file, weather, syntax), with the line of the set-up and what to do; runs with the same cause together.
* Horizons taken from the Daisy samples no longer bring the logs of their own run (the error Unknown 'log' model 'crop' is gone).
* **See all the Daisy output files**, at the bottom of Experiments: every experiment, run and file in order, in the Notepad++ viewer.
* Log plots follow the runs: new runs replace those of the previous experiment.

* **Summary of an experiment**: what was varied, the range of every result, the effect of every input on every result as a coloured table and in words; saved as summary.csv.
* **Log plots with several runs**: every run with the same log together, or two runs against each other, in any of the figures; a new *Full range over time* view.

* **Log plots: twenty kinds of figure** in six groups (over time, totals and means, the seasons, distribution, relationship, over depth), each explained in one line, with only the controls it needs.

* **View / edit the Daisy outputs**: every file Daisy wrote (.dlf logs, daisy.log) opens in a full-screen editor in the style of Notepad++ (tabs, line numbers, find), one file at a time or all runs together; the edited text is used in every save and the text Daisy wrote is kept beside it.
* The guide of Experiments starts with choosing a ready experiment (Best combinations, By topic, Prepared experiments, Import, Load this experiment).

* Daisy runs are kept in **SWAFL runs next to Daisy** (the Daisy folder when it can be written, otherwise next to the Daisy of SWAFL), no longer in Documents.
* Figures of every log: **monthly, yearly and cumulative** besides through the year and every day; the Log plots tab has monthly and cumulative values too.
* **Compare similar runs** (Log plots): the same log of several runs in one figure and each run minus a reference.
* **Which combination is best**: every combination ranked with weights the person sets, and the best value of each input.
* **All the outputs of every run**: every run in a folding group, files ticked one by one or by kind, saved together or all at once.
* Results in **folding groups**.
* Five new **soil hydraulic sensitivity factors**: K_sat, alpha, n, Theta_sat and l of the van Genuchten-Mualem curves of every horizon.
* The **request box, *Make a plan from my request* and the online AI are removed** from Experiments: plans start from the Daisy reference (best combinations, topics), the prepared experiments, the experiments of the study or imported set-ups.

* **Every log of every run**: every quantity of every log Daisy wrote becomes a journal-style figure (panels by one factor, lines by another, a band for the spread): daily logs through the year or over the whole period, annual logs year by year, depth logs as depth-time maps and profiles.
* Experiments write daily crop, water and soil-water logs by default, so the time series are always there.
* **Save both**: the outputs of the experiment and the raw files of Daisy (with the set-up) in one .zip.
* The table of raw files names each run once.

* The figures of an experiment begin with **every run as one row** (each combination with its own results) and **the main effect of each factor** with its standard error and number of runs. When only some combinations were run, the figures by factor give way to the run-by-run figures.
* Water and nitrogen balances are drawn **run by run** as stacked bars when the design is not complete.
* The processes of an experiment or a scenario are a **three-column diagram**: factors, the processes of Daisy involved in the colours of Fig. 1, results.
* No figure says *not drawn*: figures and process chips without data are left out.
* In the layers D1 to D8, the runs of an experiment are named by **short labels** of their values; every label is cut to its room with an ellipsis, chips wrap by their measured width, and the tags of the scatter plots are placed where they do not touch a point or another tag.

## The designs

* Inputs are **value boxes** with exact values, units and a list of the allowed range (Section 18.2).
* **Nothing runs while you type**: results appear after **Run simulation** and are withdrawn when an input or a setting changes (Section 18.1).
* **Find the most efficient inputs**, with your present inputs competing (Section 18.4); the surface search also tries shorter runs.
* **Field size** as an area or as a length and a width, for drip and sprinklers (Section 18.3).
* The **field sketch** is a guided drawing: corners you drag, side lengths you type, the source and the inlet, the pipe layout drawn as you choose, and seven steps of questions (field, water, crop, soil, system, running, summary) (Chapter 20). **Compare the irrigation systems** (Section 18.7).
* Each design ends with a **film of the soil water** covering the days ahead and the rest of the season. Every day is coloured by its phase (irrigation, rain, comfortable, nearing the trigger, stress, near wilting, no crop). The film shows the root zone drying towards the trigger and, without irrigation, towards the wilting point, and gives the totals and the share of days in each phase (Chapter 20). The schedule takes its depths and times from the design.
* The seasonal layers have **one dashboard chart per part of the layer**, with how each quantity varies over the twenty seasons (Chapter 14).
* *Trigger law* and *Mulch against bare soil* stand still until **Play**; the season plays at two days a second.
* Values may be typed up to the **physical limits** of the system, beyond the usual range of the list (Section 18.2).
* A surface set lasts at most 48 hours; when the inflow is too small for the soil, the design says so (Chapter 11).
* The surface layer iterates the advance exponent in the steady-intake term (Chapter 11); the slow-advance cases of the textbook example are now within 2 %.

## The data and the models

* **Weather**: import of daily weather files and the online weather of a place, with every layer following (Chapter 19).
* **Crops**: 48 crops with their FAO-56 coefficients; the *Crop water requirement* layer (Chapter 21).
* **Seasonal data**: the twenty seasons of the simulations, following every setting (Chapter 22).
* **Sensor records** follow every setting and every soil by the delta method (Eq. 6.4a).
* **Sand and loamy sand** now respond to the trigger, the sensor depth and the method (Eq. 5.12a).
* The water balance of every crop closes (the limit on evapotranspiration was raised from 2,000 to 5,000 mm).
* Yield is in Mg/ha for the simulated cereal and in per cent of the potential for a library crop, with differences in *% points*.

## The field on the map

* The field sketch has a hand to move the drawing, zoom in and out (buttons and mouse wheel), *Fit*, and a north arrow and scale bar that follow the zoom.
* The field sketch draws greenhouses and polytunnels, plant beds, areas to irrigate, no-irrigation zones, single plants and monitoring traps; the area to irrigate feeds the design; *Undo* and *Redo* (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y) cover every change (Chapter 18).
* The real field has a section 4b: terrain (contours, flow, wetness index, 3D view), watershed with Strahler orders and time of concentration, soil retention curves mapped by geostatistics with the days to the trigger cell by cell, soil water sensors fused with the satellite by regression kriging, optimised sites, and *Use this field in the DSS* (Chapter 20).
* *Tools ▸ Field on the map…* finds the farm (place search, coordinates, satellite image, Google Earth), demarcates it on the image, computes NDVI, EVI, SAVI, NDRE and NDWI of the demarcated field from Sentinel-2 or simulated by SWAFL, and exports the report (PDF or PNG at 96 to 600 dpi, with coordinates and authorship), GeoJSON and CSV (Chapter 20).
* The virtual farm simulation has been removed; the real field on the map (Chapter 20) takes its place.
* The Trigger law block of the Dashboard shows the current soil as a table, its season chart and the advice with each condition ticked.
* The index map is cut exactly to the demarcated outline on the real map and follows every change of the outline; it shows continuous colours or five management zones with their areas, two indices side by side, the value under the cursor, and the irrigation layout of the sketch.
* The soil-water film of every design follows the season of the crop in Properties (starting at its next sowing when the working date has no crop), gives the advice of when to plant over the whole year, and each design has the weather line with the button for the online weather of the farm.
* The field map makes a variable-rate irrigation map (zones of net depth from the satellite indices and the simulation, editable, exported as a prescription), the ephemeral gully risk with the predicted gullies (terrain threshold and a ridge and valley filter of the photo) and the soil loss (RUSLE); the Soil hydraulic drivers dashboard is complete.
* The field map reads the season of the real field from twelve months of Sentinel-2 (curves, change, trend, stability and monitoring zones); the variable-rate irrigation simulates every zone with its own soil (SoilGrids), its observed cover and the SWAFL model and weather of the farm, giving each zone its dates and depths; the erosion uses the daily rain of the weather in use, the runoff on that soil and the cover of every month seen by Sentinel-2.
* The irrigation schedule of every design is the FAO-56 plan of the farm: the crop coefficient Kc of every day times the reference evapotranspiration of the weather in use, the rain, the roots and the soil, the trigger, the mulch, and the net depth, efficiency and run time of the design (Eq. 18.4). The film, the schedule, the season rows of the designs, the Dashboard and the zone-by-zone irrigation of the field map use it, and every control changes it.
* The field map keeps every computed map as a layer (*Maps of this field*) to show again without computing; the site keeps its results when the window is opened again; the map moves to a water source only on **Show**.
* The Soil hydraulic drivers layer shows its whole height, down to the notes under the soil profiles.
* The web explorer of the repository is built from the engine of version 1.1.0 (tools/build_explorer.py).
* The new layer **Deterministic phenotyping** (a layer group of its own in the Layers panel, after Soil structure and tillage, Chapter 8) is a virtual phenotyping experiment: one plant of maize, sorghum, wheat or common bean grown twice (well-watered and drought) in the soil, tillage, weather and rain of the project, with a camera, thermal, X-ray and soil water view that can be turned, traits through time, the water and carbon paths, the soil that the root tips meet, twelve genotypes that relate root traits to shoot traits, a table of 26 traits, and the loading, comparison, calibration and export of your own measurements.
* The layer has a **drought stress calendar** (periods of stress with their dates, growth stage, lowest water and what each does to the roots, for the past and for a dry, typical or wet future); every time chart of the layer shows calendar dates with a line on the date of the toolbar; the date of the toolbar is the first day of every figure and the day shown, the control *Season starts* and the control *Time axis* (the season, or a year from the date) set the rest; the new section *Weather in use* shows the weather file, the online weather or the model weather that the layer reads; the water chart keeps rain and irrigation in a band of their own (Chapter 8)
* **Daisy 7.1.14 for Windows is built into SWAFL** (the folder `daisy` of the zip, copied into the program by the build): the Daisy runs start by themselves, nothing has to be installed or chosen; the Daisy installer file is recognised and ignored if it was chosen as the program
* After **Run simulation** every layer keeps playing, and starts again at the end of its season, until **Pause** is pressed (the designs, the other layers and the layer Deterministic phenotyping)
* The layer Deterministic phenotyping has a section **Drought and the roots at a glance**: time series of the water of the root zone, the root growth and the root depth with the exact date on which each period of drought stress begins
* In the Layers panel the **Daisy model** and all its groups start closed and open only when clicked; *Collapse all* closes them too
* Every layer has a **Run simulation** bar and shows its figures only after it is pressed for the present controls; the toolbar Play button does the same. The Deterministic phenotyping layer has its Run simulation button under its inputs, follows the date of the toolbar (and shows the time of day when it is today), shows no equations on the screen (they are all in Chapter 8), and is connected to the crop library, soil, weather, date, mulch, irrigation trigger, sensor and method, nitrogen and salt of the Properties panel, has collapsible sections, a section *Roots day by day*, a prediction of the phenotype and of the genotype of the library, a comparison and a fit to the SWAFL Daisy runs (E2, E7, E13, E14), the equations P.1 to P.26 explained one by one (Chapter 8), life-like plants with a **season film** (both plants and roots at eight dates, a play button that grows them from sowing to maturity, and the time series below), a bar of jump buttons that stays at the top of the window, and a **Drought outlook from the date of the toolbar**: the plant grown to the date on the recorded weather and then on the forecast and on a dry, a typical and a wet future taken from the years of the weather record, with the root zone water, the days of stress, the grain, the next watering and the drought risk.
