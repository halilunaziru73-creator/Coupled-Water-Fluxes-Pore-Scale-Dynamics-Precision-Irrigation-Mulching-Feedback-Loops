# Results and analysis {#daisy-results}

The third group draws what a run produced.

## The five scenarios, with their results

The screens that follow were taken after a real Daisy run of each scenario on the built-in farm with the built-in Taastrup weather for the years 1990 to 1993. The screens in this chapter show the figures as they were first drawn; the figures are now drawn in the style described at the end of this chapter (a title at the top left, light grid, colour blind safe colours, the legend below the figure, a colour bar for the maps of depth against time), and the numbers in them are the same.

## Processes, plots, balance and tests

### Processes: the map

Processes is a map of the model after Fig. 1 of the Daisy paper. Each box is a process; clicking it explains it and draws its figures.

**The original figure, or every component in one figure.** The *Figure* box above the map chooses between two drawings. *Fig. 1 of the Daisy paper (original)*, the default, is the map as before, with the components that Fig. 1 has no box for as buttons under it. *Fig. 1 with every component of Daisy (standalone)* is the same figure, unchanged above the ground line, with every other component of Daisy drawn under the profile as a box of the same kind as the boxes of the map (the same rectangles, colours and serif labels), in one row group for each module: water and the surface (11), heat (3), nitrogen (1), organic matter and carbon (6), crop (10), management (7), chemicals and solutes (8), and soil and numerics (9). Ten of them come from the reference manual and had no box or button before: vernalization, rubisco nitrogen in the canopy, the water stress effect on production, vegetation without a crop model, reactions and chemical equilibria, maximal exfiltration, geometry and 2D movement, programs and batch runs, summaries and measured data, and units. ### Run comparison: the verdict and the table

Run comparison puts two runs side by side and says, in plain words, how they differ. Choose run A and run B: the logs of two runs (two scenarios, the same set-up before and after a change, two variants of an experiment), or a run and a table of measurements. The comparison starts as soon as both are chosen; the arrows between them swap A and B.

- **Matching.** The rows are matched by date, so two runs of different lengths are compared on their common dates; logs without dates are matched row by row.
- **The verdict.** *Identical*, *The same within the meaningful level* or *Different*, with the number of quantities of each kind and the three largest relative differences. A quantity whose mean is zero in A (irrigation of a rainfed run) is reported with its two means.
- **The smallest meaningful level (SML).** The difference below which Daisy's own test suite (daisypy-test) counts two values as the same, for every column of every standard log (1 mm for the water balance). A difference within it is *below the meaningful level*; one under a thousandth of it is *identical* (the share is set under *Tolerances*). Quantities without an SML are compared with the absolute tolerance.

The table gives, for every quantity, its unit, the verdict, the SML, the means of A and B, the mean difference $\overline{B-A}$ and the same in per cent of A, the root mean square difference, the largest difference and its date, the mean absolute difference, the Kling-Gupta efficiency KGE, the correlation $r$ and the Nash-Sutcliffe efficiency of B on A,

$$
\mathrm{NSE}=1-\frac{\sum_t (B_t-A_t)^2}{\sum_t (A_t-\overline{A})^2},
$$

which is 1 when B equals A and below 0 when B is further from A than the mean of A is. *Show only the different ones* shortens the table. *Compare same-named logs of two folders* compares every log that two loaded folders have in common and gives one verdict line for each.

\newpage

$$
\mathrm{KGE}=1-\sqrt{(r-1)^2+\Bigl(\frac{\sigma_B}{\sigma_A}-1\Bigr)^2+\Bigl(\frac{\overline B}{\overline A}-1\Bigr)^2}
$$

is 1 when B has the same correlation, spread and mean as A (Gupta et al., 2009). *Save this table (CSV)* writes the table.

### Double-blind comparison

A judgement of two runs is easily coloured by knowing which is which (the new set-up, the expected answer). The double-blind comparison hides it from both judges, you and the program:

1. **Choose the two runs** (any log in Files, or any log of a run of Experiments or Scenarios, listed as *run : log*) and, if you have them, the **measurements** to judge against (a log or a table with the same column names).
2. **Start.** The program puts the runs behind the letters X and Y by a random draw and shows a fingerprint (the first 12 hexadecimal digits of the SHA-256 hash) of the hidden key, which holds the two names and a random salt. Neither the figures nor the numbers show the names, and the quantities come in a random order. Quantities that are identical in the two runs are left out.
3. **Judge.** For each quantity, read its three figures (X and Y, Y - X, Y against X) and press *X is better*, *Y is better* or *no real difference*. With measurements the program judges too, without the names: the run with the smaller root mean square error against the measurements is better, unless the two errors are within 2 % of each other.
4. **Reveal.** The names behind X and Y appear, the fingerprint is computed again from the key to show that it was not changed, and a table gives each quantity with your judgement, the program's and whether they agree, with the share of agreement. *Save the judgements (CSV)* keeps the record.

## How the figures are drawn

Every figure of the workbench, in the scenarios, the processes, the experiments and the tabs Plot and Soil column, is drawn in the style of a journal figure and in the style of the figures of the SWAFL repository. The title is at the top left in bold. The panel is white with a light grid and the axes at the left and at the bottom only. A time axis has a tick for every year, and a run of less than a year has ticks for the months. The colours are the colour blind safe set (blue, orange, green, pink, yellow, sky blue), the legend is below the figure, and a map of depth against time has a colour bar with the unit written in the log file itself. Nothing but the title, the axes, the legend and the data is drawn: no citation and no caption is added to a figure, to a saved picture or to a saved report.

What each figure shows is written under its name, with the Daisy log it is drawn from, so that every figure can be traced to a file. A figure that has several panels is saved as one picture with all of its panels.

* The yield figure shows the storage organ at each harvest, coloured by crop, and below it the storage organ against the stem in the way of the daisyrVis exercise of the Daisy course.
* The water figures show the annual balance (rain and irrigation as bars, evapotranspiration and drainage as lines) and the running totals through the whole run.
* The water content and the suction are maps of depth against time. In the suction map the black marks FC and WP are field capacity (pF 2) and the wilting point (pF 4.2).
* The crop figure shows the leaf area index, the water and nitrogen stress and the dry matter of the plant parts. The stress of Daisy is 0 when there is none and 1 when the crop cannot grow.
* The nitrogen figure shows where the fertiliser goes (crop uptake, leaching below 100 cm, gaseous loss by denitrification and N2O, volatilisation) against the fertiliser applied and the mineralisation.

A column of a log that is zero throughout is left out of a process figure, and the note under the figure says which. A figure is never left empty without a sentence that says why.

## The raw output of Daisy

Under the figures of a scenario, and under *One run in detail* of an experiment, the panel **The raw output of Daisy** gives the files exactly as Daisy wrote them, before any figure or summary of SWAFL.

1. **Kept on this computer.** The desktop app keeps the whole folder of every run in *SWAFL runs*, next to the Daisy that runs it: in the Daisy folder itself when it can be written, otherwise next to the Daisy that SWAFL keeps for itself (*%LOCALAPPDATA%/SWAFL/Daisy-7.1.14/SWAFL runs*). Runs of earlier versions in *Documents / SWAFL Daisy runs* are still listed and read. A scenario makes one folder named after the scenario and the time, with one sub-folder for each run. An experiment makes one folder with one sub-folder for each run, named by its number and its factors. The sub-folder *Run* holds the logs (.dlf), the .csv files and daisy.log, and the set-up that made them (Runfile.dai and the files it includes). The button *Open the folder* opens it in the file explorer. The folders stay until you delete them.
2. **Download all raw outputs (.zip).** One zip with the logs of all the runs of the scenario, untouched, and a README.
3. **Download outputs and set-up (.zip).** The same, with the files that were given to Daisy, so that the run can be repeated on any computer with Daisy 7.1.
4. **The list of files.** One row for each file with what it holds, the number of rows and columns, the size and a Download button.

A .dlf file is a text table: header lines (VERSION, LOGFILE, RUN, SIM...), a line of dashes, the column names, the units and then the numbers, separated by tabs. It opens in Excel or in any text editor, in R with the function read_dlf of the package daisyrVis, in Python with daisypy-io, and in SWAFL itself in the tab Files. The figures are drawn from these files only, so a figure can always be checked against its file.


## The efficiency indices

Every scenario that has what an index needs shows it in the summary table and in the figure "Efficiency indices". Irrigation efficiency (IE) is the yield divided by the irrigation applied, in kg of yield per m3 of water (1 mm is 10 m3 per ha). Irrigation water use efficiency (IWUE) is the yield of the irrigated run minus the yield of the rainfed run, divided by the irrigation applied, in kg per m3; it needs a rainfed run, which the experiments add for every combination of the other factors. The deep percolation fraction (DPF) is the drainage below 100 cm divided by the irrigation plus the rainfall, in percent. The nitrogen efficiency is the partial factor productivity of nitrogen (PFPn), the yield divided by the nitrogen applied, in kg DM per kg N. The nitrogen leaching fraction (NLF) is the nitrogen leached below 100 cm divided by the nitrogen applied, in percent. An index that cannot be computed, for example IE without irrigation, is left out and not shown as zero.

Panel letters (a, b, c) mark the panels of the figures that have several.

## The figures of one experiment

Every experiment of the study (E0 to E16) has its own question, its own design and its own figures, and the figures are drawn again from the runs each time results arrive, so they grow while the runs finish. Load an experiment with *Experiments of the study* at the top of the Experiments tab, then press *Run all*.

* The panel **Figures of this experiment** states the question and the design, and then asks which figures are wanted. All are ticked at first; untick the ones that are not needed, or open *Add a figure* and choose the kind (lines, bars, a surface over two factors, where the water goes, where the nitrogen goes, which factor matters most), the factor on the x axis, the factor that sets the colour, the factor that splits the figure into panels (a, b, c, ...) and the response.
* Lines run with a colour bar when the colour factor is numeric (for example the duration of one irrigation). A surface is drawn smooth with white contour lines carrying their values, the tested combinations as dots and a colour bar. Panels of one figure share one scale, so they can be compared.
* An experiment of your own, or one that is changed, gets figures that follow its factors: the numeric factor with the most levels goes on the x axis, the next factor sets the colour and the third splits the panels.
* **Every run is one row.** The first figure, *The results of every run* (panel a), has one row for each run, named by its own values in short (for example *-14 d · N×0.7 · Sand · +2 °C · irrigated*), and one panel for each result (yield, irrigation, percolation, nitrate leached), each with its own scale and a dashed line at the mean of the runs. Runs are ordered by yield; rainfed references are open circles. Every combination therefore has its own result, side by side, and nothing is averaged away.
* **Main effects with their error.** The second figure, *Main effect of each factor* (panel b), has one panel for each factor that changes: the mean of the runs at each value, one standard error and the number of runs (n) under each value, on one y scale shared by the panels, with the mean of all runs as a dashed line. It is the figure of a screening or factorial design in a journal.
* **Complete or not.** The note above the figures says whether every combination of the values was run. When it was, the figures of the experiment follow (lines, bars, surfaces by factor). When only some combinations were run (the number of runs was smaller than the number of combinations), lines and surfaces by factor would join runs that differ in other values as well, so they are not drawn: the figures are then every run, the main effects, the effect ranking and the water and nitrogen balances run by run (one stacked bar for each run).
* **Style.** The figures follow the conventions of journal figures: grey facet strips over the panels, panel letters, the Okabe-Ito colours (readable by people with colour blindness), a light panel background with a fine grid, units in every axis title, and a one-line legend under the figure. Labels that are longer than their room are shortened with an ellipsis, never written over each other.
* **No empty figures.** A figure whose logs a run did not write is left out, without a message; a process opens only the figures that can be drawn.
* Below the figures, **the processes of Daisy in this experiment** are drawn as a diagram of three columns: on the left what the experiment changes (F1, F2, in orange frames), in the middle only the processes of Daisy it involves, in the fills of Fig. 1 of Daisy (blue for water, green for heat, the crop and residues, beige for nitrogen, brown for organic matter), and on the right what it measures (R1, R2, in blue frames). One arrow joins a factor to each process it acts on and a process to each result that comes from it; nothing else is drawn. A process that both receives a factor and gives a result has an orange frame inside a blue one. Click a process and the figures that depend on it light up; click it again to release. The scenarios draw the same diagram for their own factors and results.
* Every figure has the same toolbar: collapse, *Titles* (the title, the axis texts and the panel letter can be written as the paper needs them), and *Save* as a picture at screen resolution, 300 dpi or 600 dpi, copy, or the numbers behind the figure as a CSV table. A figure of several panels is saved as one picture.
* **Each experiment keeps its own outputs.** The runs of one experiment go to one folder named by the experiment, its title and the time (for example E5_Tillage-system-by-consolidation_2026-10-29-21-25), never mixed with another experiment. When the runs have finished, the program writes into that folder an *Outputs* folder with README.txt (the question and the design), plan.json (to load the experiment again), design.csv (every run and its factor levels), results.csv (the responses of every run) and a folder *figures* with every figure as a 300 dpi picture and its numbers. The button *Save the outputs of this experiment* writes them again, and *as a zip* downloads them.

## Efficiencies, budgets and trade-offs, from the literature {#daisy-eff}

Under every experiment, and under every scenario with three or more runs, the group **Efficiencies, budgets and trade-offs, from the literature** draws 21 figures from the results measured in the runs. Nothing is typed in: every number comes from the logs of the runs, and every figure has the toolbar of every figure (*Titles*, *Save* as a picture at 300 or 600 dpi, copy, or the numbers behind it as a CSV table). The figures are drawn when they come into view, so the page opens at once. Point at a dot or a bar for its numbers. The box *Colour the runs by* at the top sets the quantity that gives the colour of the dots and lines in all figures, and *Second objective of the trade-off* sets the second axis of the Pareto figure. A figure whose quantities the runs did not measure is left out, without a message.

**The indices.** They are added to the results of every run, so they can also be chosen as the result of the whole experiment (Section \ref{daisy-best}).

| Result | Formula | Source |
|:--------|:------------|:--------|
| Nitrogen use efficiency, NUE (%) | $100 \times$ crop uptake of N $/$ N input | EU Nitrogen Expert Panel 2015 |
| Nitrogen surplus (kg N per ha) | N input $-$ crop uptake of N | EU Nitrogen Expert Panel 2015 |
| Reactive nitrogen lost | nitrate leached + denitrified + volatilised N | n/a |
| Nitrate leached per Mg of yield | nitrate leached $/$ yield | van Groenigen et al. 2010 |
| Agronomic efficiency, AE (kg per kg N) | (yield $-$ yield of the least fertilised run) $/$ extra N applied | Dobermann 2005 |
| Apparent recovery, RE (%) | $100 \times$ extra uptake $/$ extra N applied | Dobermann 2005 |
| Physiological efficiency, PE (kg per kg N) | yield gain $/$ extra N taken up (AE = RE $\times$ PE $/$ 100) | Dobermann 2005 |
| Water-use efficiency (kg per m3) | yield $/$ evapotranspiration | French and Schultz 1984 |
| Yield per m3 of rain and irrigation | yield $/$ (rain + irrigation) | Howell 2001 |
| Harvest index | storage organ $/$ total dry matter | n/a |
| Relative evapotranspiration | actual $/$ potential | Doorenbos and Kassam 1979 |
| Nitrate in the drainage (mg N per L) | $100 \times$ nitrate leached $/$ drainage | EU Nitrates Directive 91/676/EEC (limit 11.3 mg N per L) |
| Chemical leached, degraded (%) | share of the amount sprayed | n/a |

**Nitrogen.**

1. *Nitrogen budget of every run*: a bar for every run, split into crop uptake, nitrate leached, denitrified, volatilised and the rest left in the soil, ranked by surplus.
2. *Nitrogen use efficiency against nitrogen surplus*: the diagram of the EU Nitrogen Expert Panel. The green box is the target (NUE 50 to 90 % and a surplus below 80 kg N per ha); left of it nitrogen is applied in excess, right of it the soil stock is mined.
3. *Yield against nitrogen use efficiency*, with the line of best fit.
4. *Nitrogen response curve*: yield against the nitrogen applied with a quadratic fit $y = a + bx + cx^2$, its R squared and, when the curve bends down, the rate of maximum yield.
5. *Agronomic efficiency, recovery and physiological efficiency*: three panels sharing the rows.
6. *Nitrate leached against nitrogen surplus*, with the line of best fit: the slope is the share of the surplus that leaves as nitrate.
7. *Nitrate in the drainage water*: nitrate leached against drainage with the lines of 5, 11.3 and 20 mg N per L; runs above the 11.3 line exceed the limit of the Nitrates Directive.
8. *Pathways of the reactive nitrogen lost*: the share of leaching, denitrification and volatilisation; a shift between them is pollution swapping.
9. *Nitrate leached per tonne of yield* against the nitrogen applied.

**Water.**

10. *Water balance of every run*: evapotranspiration, drainage, runoff and the change in storage as shares of rain plus irrigation.
11. *Water production function*: yield against evapotranspiration with a line; its slope is the water productivity per extra millimetre.
12. *Yield response factor Ky*: $1 - Y_a/Y_m = K_y\,(1 - ET_a/ET_m)$, the relative yield decrease against the relative evapotranspiration deficit with the dashed 1:1 line; the slope of the fitted line is Ky.
13. *Irrigation water productivity* against irrigation, when the experiment has a rainfed run to compare with.
14. *Water stress against nitrogen stress*: the days of each stress before harvest; runs above the dashed line lack nitrogen first, runs below it lack water first.
15. *Water and nitrogen efficiency together*: water-use efficiency against NUE with the front of the runs no other run beats on both.

**All results together.**

16. *Correlation of the results*: Pearson r between every pair of results (red: rise together, blue: one rises while the other falls).
17. *Trade-off and Pareto front*: yield against a second objective of your choice; the red line joins the runs that no other run beats on both counts.
18. *All results of every run side by side*: parallel coordinates, each axis from the lowest to the highest value.
19. *Which factor moves the result most*: for each factor the mean result at its lowest and highest value against the mean of all runs, ranked by the length of the bar.
20. *Distribution of a result*: histogram and cumulative curve with the quartiles.
21. *Combined score of the runs*: every result scaled from the worst to the best run (inverted where less is better) and averaged with equal weights; stacked bars show what each result contributes.

Two more figures complete the group. *Dose-response of each factor on the main results* draws, for every factor, the mean of each main result at each value, scaled from 0 to 1 so that results in different units share one axis. For chemicals, *GUS leaching index* sets the simulated leaching against $GUS = \log_{10}(DT_{50})\,(4 - \log_{10} K_{OC})$ of Gustafson 1989: a chemical above 2.8 is a likely leacher and one below 1.8 a non-leacher.

## Interactions and time series, for the paper {#daisy-adv}

The group *Interactions and time series, for the paper* has five figures for the results of one experiment.

* **Interaction map of the factors.** Each cell is the share of the variance of the results explained by one factor alone (the diagonal, blue) or by a pair of factors beyond their separate effects (red, an interaction). Click a cell to open the interaction plot of that pair.
* **Interaction plot.** The mean result at each value of one factor, one line for each value of another, with the lowest and highest run as a whisker; the same numbers with the two factors swapped are in panel (b). Lines that are not parallel mean an interaction.
* **Effect of a factor through the year.** For matched pairs of runs that differ only in that factor, the difference of a daily quantity day by day, one line for each value of the other factor, with the band of 10 to 90 %.
* **Every run through the year.** One line for each run, coloured by its final result, with the lowest and highest run in black.
* **Runs against days.** One row for each run, ranked by the result, one column for each day of the year, coloured by the quantity.

The quantity is chosen in the box *Quantity* (the daily logs of the runs) and can be shown as the value or as the running total. The group is drawn from the logs, so it needs runs with daily logs, or at least two factors.

## Where the results are saved: you choose the folder {#daisy-save-folder}

SWAFL does not write the results of experiments to a hidden folder any more. While the runs are made, the files are kept in a temporary place that is shown only as a note; nothing is saved to the computer by itself. When the runs have finished, *Save the outputs of this experiment* and *Save both: outputs and raw Daisy files* ask for a folder with the usual window of the computer, and write into the folder chosen. *Open folder* shows what was saved. If the window is closed without a choice, nothing is written and the results stay on the screen for another try.

## Hover texts and the details of the measured process map

The process map of an experiment or a scenario now draws every measured result with the range of its values in the runs (for example *yield 4.1 to 8.9 Mg per ha*) and every factor with its levels, and the result selected above the map is outlined. For a scenario the map shows only the factors that the variants you ran changed and the results measured in the logs of those runs, not a generic list. Every button and every figure has a hover text that says what it does.

## Every log of every run {#daisy-every-log}

Under the figures of an experiment, the panel **Every log of every run** turns everything Daisy wrote into figures: every log of the runs (the daily and the annual logs, and every log of the Daisy library that was added to the runs) and every quantity in it, one figure each. Nothing has to be chosen first; the quantities a paper usually shows come first (leaf area index, the storage organ, the development stage, evapotranspiration, transpiration, soil evaporation, percolation and leaching, crop nitrogen, nitrate, stress, water content), then all the others.

* **The layout of a journal figure.** One panel for each value of one factor (*Panels by*), one line for each value of a second factor (*Lines by*, or *every run* for one line per run), the other factors averaged over the runs that share these two values. All panels have one y scale and are lettered (a), (b), (c); the grey strip over a panel names its value; the legend is written once under the panels. Lines for numbers in three or more steps follow a colour ramp (viridis), other lines the Okabe-Ito colours.
* **Daily logs** are drawn *through the year* (the default): for every day of the year, the mean (or the median, *Line*) of all seasons and runs on that day, with 10 to 90 % of them as the shaded band, so the typical season and its spread are seen at once. *Over the whole period* draws every day of the run instead, with the lowest and the highest run as the band.
* **Annual logs** are drawn year by year, with open circles on the seasons and the lowest to the highest run as the band. The first record of an annual log (the start of the run, zero) is left out.
* **Depth logs** (water content, pressure, nitrate, temperature at each depth) give two figures: a map of depth (down) against time, one map for each value of the panel factor with one colour scale for all, and the profile, the mean at each depth with 10 to 90 % of the days and runs as the band.
* *Log* shows the figures of one log, and the search box finds a quantity by its name (LAI, nitrate, evaporation). The note above every figure says what it shows: the log, the statistic, the band, and the factors that were averaged.
* Every figure has the same toolbar as the others (titles, picture at screen resolution, 300 or 600 dpi, the numbers as a CSV table). *Save these figures (.zip)* saves all of them; they are also saved with the outputs of the experiment.

The daily figures need daily logs. **Results saved** under the plan is now *yearly totals and daily crop, water and soil water* by default, so every experiment writes them; *yearly totals only* is faster but gives the annual figures alone.

### Monthly, yearly and cumulative

*Time* above the figures of every log has five choices: *through the year* (the mean of the seasons, day by day), *every day of the period*, *monthly*, *yearly* and *cumulative over all the years*. Monthly and yearly values are the totals of the month or the year for quantities that flow (water in mm, nitrogen in kg N/ha: rain, evapotranspiration, percolation, leaching) and the means for states (leaf area, water content, dry matter); the y axis says which ("per month", "per year"). *Cumulative* is the running total of every run from the start of the run, so the runs part as their totals grow apart. Annual logs offer *yearly* and *cumulative*. The *Log plots* tab has the same choices for one log (*Monthly values*, *Annual values*, *Cumulative over all the years*).

The figures of each log are a group of their own that folds open and shut (the first is open), and the results of an experiment are in folding groups too: *Figures of this experiment*, *Which combination is best*, *Effects, interactions and trade-offs*, *Every log of every run*, *All the outputs of every run* and *One run in detail*.

## Why a run gave no results {#daisy-why-failed}

When Daisy gives no results for some runs, a panel **Why N runs gave no results** opens under the progress bar, and the list of runs names the cause of each one. The runs that failed for the same reason are put together, so every cause is explained once:

* **Daisy said**: the error line of Daisy, exactly.
* **Where**: the file, the line and the column, and the lines around it from the set-up of the run, with the place marked.
* **Root cause**, in plain words, sorted into one of these kinds: *a name Daisy does not know* (a crop, a log, a horizon used but not defined: often a file of the Daisy samples that still refers to its own run), *a numerical problem* (the solver of the Richards equation or of the solutes did not converge, or a value became not a number or negative: nearly always an extreme combination of soil hydraulic values, thin layers or a very large irrigation flux), *a value outside what Daisy accepts*, *a file that is missing*, *the weather* (years or gaps), *a name defined twice*, *a line Daisy could not read*, and *Daisy stopped without a message* (cancelled, memory, an antivirus).
* **What to do**: the change that removes the cause, and the runs that share it.

For example, *test-prd.dai:292:29: Unknown 'log' model 'crop'* came from the soil KarJyn_B, a horizon of the Daisy sample test-prd.dai used as it is: the sample file also carries the logs of its own run, which need a log model SWAFL runs do not load. SWAFL now takes only the definitions from such a sample file and leaves its logs out; a run with this soil, tested with Daisy 7.1.14, finishes and writes all its logs.

## Every simulated experiment {#daisy-all-experiments}

At the bottom of the Experiments tab, **See all the Daisy output files** opens every experiment that was run, in order, in the full-screen viewer: the folder of each experiment (its name and the time), the folder of each run in it (r000, r001, ...), and in each run the files Daisy wrote, sorted (the annual logs, the daily logs, daisy.log), with their sizes. A click on a file opens it on the right in the colours of Notepad++ (line numbers, find, wrap); a file is read from the disk only when it is opened, so even hundreds of runs open at once. On the desktop the list is the folder *SWAFL runs* next to Daisy (and older runs in Documents, under *Older runs*); in the web version, the experiments of the session. *Save the folder (.zip)* saves them all.

The figures always follow the runs: when an experiment has new runs, the Log plots tab drops the runs of the previous one and loads the new ones, and the figures of every log, the summary and the ranking are drawn from the same runs.

## The summary of an experiment {#daisy-summary}

The first group of the results, **Summary: every range and its effect**, gathers the whole experiment in four parts, and *Summary (CSV)* saves it (it is also *Outputs/summary.csv* with the outputs):

1. **What was varied**: every input with the values tested.
2. **The range of every result** over all runs: lowest, highest, mean, spread (coefficient of variation) and the best and the worst run by name.
3. **The effect of every input on every result**: a table with one row per input and one column per result. For a number the effect is the change of the mean from its lowest to its highest value, for a choice from the choice with the lowest result to the one with the highest; the share of the mean is in brackets. Blue cells rise, red cells fall, and the stronger the colour the larger the effect; pointing at a cell shows the mean at every value.
4. **In short**: for every result, the input that moves it most, by how much and between which values, then the next two.

## Which combination is best {#daisy-best}

Every run of an experiment is one combination of the inputs. The group *Which combination is best* ranks all of them: each result is scaled from the worst run (0) to the best run (1), higher being better for yield, uptake and the efficiencies and lower being better for irrigation, leaching, percolation, denitrification, runoff and stress, and the score is the weighted mean. The weights are boxes over the table (yield 1, nitrate leached 1 and irrigation 0.5 at first; 0 leaves a result out), and the ranking follows every change. The three best combinations are marked, a bar shows each score, and under the table *the best value of each input* lists the values of every input in the order of the mean score of their runs, with the number of runs, followed by the best combination in words.

## All the outputs of every run {#daisy-raw-all}

The group *All the outputs of every run* lists every run of the experiment, not only one: each run folds open to its files (what each holds, rows, size, a download button). Tick files one by one, a whole run (the box in its title), or a kind of file in every run at once (*Tick this file in every run*, for example Daily-FWater.dlf), then *Save the ticked files (.zip)*; *Save everything (.zip)* saves all of them. *with the set-up of each run* adds the files that made the run, and *with the outputs of the experiment* adds the figures (300 dpi, with the numbers as CSV), tables and README of the experiment (always with *Save everything*). The .zip has *Raw/<run>/outputs*, *Raw/<run>/setup* and *Outputs/*.

## Viewing and editing the outputs of Daisy before they are saved {#daisy-view-out}

The outputs here are the outputs of the Daisy simulation: the files Daisy wrote in every run (the .dlf logs such as Annual-Harvest, Daily-FWater or Daily-Theta, and daisy.log), not the figures. There are two ways to look at them and change them before they are saved.

* **One file at a time, in the style of Notepad++.** In *All the outputs of every run* (and in *One run in detail*), every file has **View / edit**. It opens the file in an editor that fills the screen: a tab for every file opened (click a tab to go back to it, × closes it), a grey margin with the line numbers, the header lines of the log in blue, the column names, the units in purple and the numbers in orange, find (Ctrl+F), wrap and *colours* (Notepad++, VS Code light or VS Code dark). Change the text and press *Save changes* (Ctrl+S): the file is marked *edited* in the list, and every save of the page (*Save the ticked files*, *Save everything*, *Download*) takes the edited text, while the text as Daisy wrote it is kept beside it in the .zip, in *outputs/_as_written_by_Daisy/*. *Back to the text of Daisy* drops the changes, *Download this file* saves the file alone, and on the desktop *Save into the run folder* writes it to *Edited/* in the folder of the run (the file Daisy wrote is left as it was). Esc or *Close* returns to the list.
* **All of them together.** **View and edit the Daisy outputs (full screen)**, in the results bar and in *All the outputs of every run*, opens every file Daisy wrote, one folder per run, in a viewer that fills the screen: the folders and files with their sizes on the left, the file in the Notepad++ editor on the right. *Save the folder (.zip)* saves all of them with the changes; on the desktop *Write them next to the runs* writes them into *Daisy outputs* in the folder of the experiment.

## Saving the outputs and the raw files of Daisy together

The results bar has three ways to save: *Save the outputs of this experiment* (README, plan, design, results table and every figure, written next to the runs on the desktop), *as a zip*, and **Save both: outputs and raw Daisy files**, one .zip with two folders:

* *Outputs/*: everything SWAFL made from the runs, the figures of every log included, each as a picture at 300 dpi with the numbers behind it as a CSV table.
* *Raw/*: one folder for every run, with *outputs/* (the .dlf logs and daisy.log exactly as Daisy wrote them) and *setup/* (Runfile.dai and the files it includes), so every run can be repeated and every figure checked against the numbers of Daisy.

On the desktop the outputs are written next to the runs as well. In the table of the raw output of a run, the name of the run is written once, beside all of its files.

## Compare similar runs in Log plots {#daisy-cmp-similar}

Under the plot of one log, *Compare similar runs* draws the same log of several runs in one figure: the runs of an experiment, of a scenario, or loaded files that hold the same log. The runs of the session are added to the loaded logs by themselves, so the comparison is ready when the tab opens. Choose the *Log* (every kind of log that two or more runs wrote), the *Column*, the *View* (every day, through the year, monthly, yearly or cumulative, with *Sum or mean* for monthly and yearly), tick the runs, and choose a *Reference*: a second figure then shows **each run minus the reference**, the effect of what differs between the runs (the rainfed reference of an experiment is chosen by itself). Each figure saves as a picture and the numbers of all runs as one CSV table.

## The crops of the rotation

By default the farm grows spring barley, winter barley, winter rape and winter wheat (twice). In Scenarios and in Experiments the panel *Choose the crops of the rotation myself* replaces them: add any crop of the Daisy library, put the crops in the order of the rotation, and give each one its date of tillage, its date of sowing, its fertiliser (month and day with the kilograms of nitrogen per hectare, for example 04-05:160,05-05:70), the latest date of harvest and whether to harvest when the crop is ripe. The four crops of the built-in farm are tested; the others are templates (marked so in the list) whose dates and nitrogen should be checked against local practice. The nitrogen factor, the tillage, the fertiliser type and the irrigation of the scenario or the experiment act on the chosen crops in the same way.
