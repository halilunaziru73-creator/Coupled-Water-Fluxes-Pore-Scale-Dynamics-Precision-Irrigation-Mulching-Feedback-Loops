# Highlights

Three to five bullets per manuscript, at most 85 characters each (journal limit). They are kept here, separate from the manuscript PDFs.

## Paper 1: Soil Hydraulic Memory, the Recoverability of Evaporation Savings and the Closure of the Water Balance

* Hydraulic memory: how much saved evaporation drains instead of staying
* Memory fate spans 0.02 to 0.85 across ten soils; one-parameter law, LOSO RMSE 0.058
* A blind search recovers the water balance and exposes an unattributed 30% of ET
* Judged by reported soil evaporation alone, recoverability flips sign in two soils
* Positive control, null model and regime-wise tests define a discovery protocol

## Paper 2: Modeling Soil-Water-Atmosphere Interactions for Irrigation Scheduling and Design

* Every output of an offline irrigation tool is tied to a numbered equation
* 134 equations carry provenance labels: 38 literature, 18 data-driven, 78 engineered
* Meta-model of 10,190 Daisy runs ranks held-out scenarios (Spearman 0.64-0.97)
* Surface, drip and sprinkler design reproduce FAO-45 and textbook examples within 3%
* One command re-runs every check; 10^5 scenario sweeps take about 2 s

## Paper 3: A Graphical Workbench and a Verified Bridge for the Daisy Model

* A request in plain words becomes a design checked against what Daisy can vary
* Planner, 17 prepared experiments and a process map; citation kept with every figure
* A Daisy layer shows only a Daisy run, otherwise it says there is none
* Parsers agree with an independent reader to 3e-15; tracer budget closes to 0.02%
* The 792-output test found and removed a 40% defect in the denitrification function

## Paper 4: SWAFL Desktop 1.1.0, the Whole Suite

* One offline application from field sketch and Sentinel-2 to irrigation design
* Weather is a choice: benchmark record, seven stations, own file, online or DMI
* All 22 layers open and run without a script error; 48 crops, 110 combinations
* Deterministic phenotyping is repeatable and closes soil water to 4e-5 mm
* Drought pulse cuts grain 14 to 42 % across seven genotypes
