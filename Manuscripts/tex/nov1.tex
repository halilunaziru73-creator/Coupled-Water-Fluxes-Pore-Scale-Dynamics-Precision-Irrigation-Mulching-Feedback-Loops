1 & Drainage and evaporation compete for stored water; their rates follow from the van~Genuchten--Mualem functions and from stage-1/stage-2 evaporation theory \citep{vanGenuchten1980,Mualem1976,Lehmann2008,Or2013}. & \emph{Hydraulic memory}, the share of released stored water that drains, defined as a measurable quantity and predicted for a soil not used in the fit by a one-parameter rate-competition law. & Spans 0.02 to 0.85 over ten soils; ordered by $n$ ($\rho=0.95$) and $K_s$ ($\rho=0.97$); leave-one-soil-out RMSE 0.058, below every structural predictor.\\
2 & Water saved from evaporation is counted as water gained, while system-scale studies warn that savings can be illusory \citep{Perry2017,Grafton2018}. & A soil- and climate-specific \emph{recoverable share} of an evaporation saving, $\rho_R=1-\psi_D$, with the drainage that is not recovered quantified per soil. & No recovery in sand, 0.20 in loamy sand; warming shifts the drainage share by $-0.16$, a wetter climate by $+0.42$; nitrogen (negative control) has no effect.\\
3 & Equation discovery recovers laws from data \citep{Schmidt2009,Brunton2016,Udrescu2020}; energy- and water-balance closure gaps are known for flux towers \citep{Wilson2002,Foken2008}. & A blind invariant search with a permutation null and positive and negative controls, applied to the outputs of a process model, recovering its water balance and exposing an unclosed evaporative term. & Balance coefficients within 5\,\% of the exact values; an unattributed term of 164\,mm\,yr$^{-1}$ (30\,\% of ET); storage non-closure of 16\,\% in two experiments.\\
4 & Emulators make process models fast but their safe targets are rarely stated \citep{Razavi2012,Asher2015}. & A map of which flux of the balance can be emulated by a compact law and which cannot. & A three-term ET law equals gradient boosting out of sample ($R^2=0.898$ by experiment, 0.904 by soil); drainage resists every law tried.\\
5 & Savings are judged against the reported soil evaporation. & The two analyses change each other: against the reported soil evaporation alone, the drainage share of an evaporation change is misjudged. & Misjudged in most soils and of the wrong sign in two (\cref{sec:auditlink}).\\
