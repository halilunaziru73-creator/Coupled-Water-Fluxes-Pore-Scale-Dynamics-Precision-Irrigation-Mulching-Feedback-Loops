# Manuscripts

Three manuscript drafts on soil water, irrigation and the [Daisy model](https://github.com/daisy-model) (developed at the University of Copenhagen) (xelatex + bibtex; `tex/build.sh p1|p2|p3`):

1. **Soil Hydraulic Memory, the Recoverability of Evaporation Savings and the Closure of the Water Balance** (`pdf/Paper1_*`): two analyses of the same 10,190-run ensemble that depend on each other. Hydraulic memory and the rate-competition law say how much of an evaporation saving stays in the root zone; the audit of the water balance (a blind linear-invariant search with a permutation null, an unattributed term of 30 % of ET, a three-term ET law) says which outputs of the model can carry that conclusion. The two are joined: against the reported soil evaporation alone, the drainage share of an evaporation change differs by more than 0.1 in six of ten soils and changes sign in two.
2. **Modeling Soil–Water–Atmosphere Interactions for Irrigation Scheduling and Design** (`pdf/Paper2_*`): the software paper of the engines. It sets out the coupled soil–water–atmosphere system (compartments, state variables, fluxes), a ledger of 134 numbered equations with their origin, the meta-model, the daily model and the closed-form design modules for surface, drip and sprinkler irrigation, their verification against published sources and held-out simulations, and the interface of version 1.1.0.
3. **Soil-Water Modelling and Decision Support through a Graphical User Interface for the Daisy Model** (`pdf/Paper3_*`): the whole suite in one paper (introduction, materials and methods, results, discussion). The layers, the tools for the real field, weather as a choice, the crop library and deterministic phenotyping; the workbench with its planner, prepared experiments, decision base, process map, soil column and calibration; the bridge that hands Daisy runs to the decision layers under one rule of origin; the checks of each part and what was not tested.
4. **The Reach of Suction-Triggered Irrigation and the Marginal Fate of Irrigation Water: Closed Forms from the van Genuchten-Mualem Parameters** (`pdf/Paper4_*`): a closed form, from alpha, n and the Mualem connectivity, for the largest suction a crop can impose at a sensor (beyond it a trigger never fires; a 300 cm trigger leaves sand and loamy sand rainfed), validated leaving one soil out within a factor of 2.3; and the marginal fate of each irrigation millimetre across soils, doses, seasons and methods (0.38 transpired, 0.44 evaporated, 0.16 drained; a soil-independent dose scale of about 4 mm). Script `reproducibility/analysis/p4_reach.py`; data `Data/Comprehensive_Summary`.


Earlier drafts had six papers; the two analyses of Paper 1 and the workbench, the bridge and the whole suite of Paper 3 were merged so that each paper carries one complete argument. Papers 1 and 2 were revised for release 1.1.0 (data statement, regenerated numbers, companion-paper pointer; the only changed number is the Engine 2 closure maximum, 0.78 to 0.70 %, because the application now runs on the date of the toolbar).

Figures in `figures/` (`shots/` full-resolution interface captures, `shots_jpg/` the compressed copies of the version 1.1.0 interface typeset in the software paper), analysis scripts in `reproducibility/analysis/`, numeric outputs in `results/`. The raw run table and the Zenodo link are in the top-level [`Data`](../Data) folder.

* `graphical_abstracts/` holds the graphical abstracts (kept out of the manuscript PDFs).
* `reproducibility/` is a self-contained package that regenerates every number and figure from the archived Daisy ensemble (`bash run_all.sh`), with pinned versions, fixed seeds, SHA-256 checksums and automatic comparison against the reported values. Start with `reproducibility/README_REPRODUCE.md`.
* Figure 1 of each manuscript uses a brown, orange, pink, rust and purple scheme; data figures use the Okabe-Ito palette.

**Status / caveats.** Drafts. No funding was received. References beyond the author's list were entered from memory; DOIs not independently verified. No field validation is claimed; negative results are reported in the text.

Paper 3 (revised 1 November 2026): Daisy 7.1.14 was run end to end (Windows build under Wine) for the library scenarios, deficit irrigation, the new soil-hydraulic sensitivity factors and a horizon of the Daisy samples, but not for every factor; the interface was tested in a browser with real Daisy outputs, not in the desktop application on Windows; the satellite and open-data services were not reachable (test data only); no field validation; new references were entered from memory and their DOIs are not verified. The revision adds the experiment engine (exact run budget, soil-hydraulic sensitivity, summary of effects, journal-style figures of every log, diagnosis of failed runs), the end-to-end runs, and screen captures of the 1.1.0 interface; the online assistant was removed from the software and from the paper. Paper 3 now also shows the satellite and open-data chain on a real 5.85 ha field near Zaria, Nigeria (Sentinel-2 scene of 30 October 2026, OpenStreetMap, Copernicus DEM, SoilGrids), replacing the figure made on a generated test image, and the season film of the phenotyping layer. Paper 2 shows the 1.1.0 captures of the surface, drip, mulch, evaporation, tillage and trigger-law layers, the field balance and crop water requirement charts, and the pore-scale drying under two tillage systems.

All three papers (revised 1 November 2026) now discuss the software through its technical manual (cited as HaliluManual, 1,154 pages, with the pages of the relevant chapters) and end their discussion with a *Novelty in one view* subsection. Paper 1 adds a section on reproducing and extending the analysis in the workbench and a figure from two real Daisy 7.1.14 runs of the loam with the van Genuchten-Mualem parameters as given and scaled (`reproducibility/fig_vg_check.py`); it is one pair of runs, an illustration and not a test of the law. Paper 2 adds the correspondence of its engines with the chapters of the manual and a figure of the plot types of the workbench.


All four papers (revised 2 November 2026) link the full per-experiment outputs in [`Data/Comprehensive_Summary`](../Data/Comprehensive_Summary) in their data statements and show a table of the 17 experiments and a figure of their outputs (script `reproducibility/analysis/data_overview.py`). Paper 4 is simulation-only (one weather series, one crop rotation, equilibrium Richards flow); its new references were entered from memory and have no verified DOIs.

The data of every table and figure of the four papers, with the script that makes each, are in [`Data/Manuscript_Data`](../Data/Manuscript_Data) (Excel workbook and map). Revised 2 November 2026: abstracts without numbers or equations, no short subsections (short ones became run-in paragraphs), soil legends on every figure of Paper 4.

Each paper has a Supplementary Material in [`pdf/Supplementary`](pdf/Supplementary) (sources `tex/s1.tex` to `tex/s4.tex`): the derivation of every equation, why each result happens, the data of the analyses (made by `reproducibility/analysis/make_supplement_data.py`) and the origin of every table and figure. The papers are independent of one another and do not refer to each other.

Revised 2 November 2026 (late): no figure is shared between papers (the data overview figure and table only in Paper 1, Paper 4 has its own table of the experiments it uses); Paper 3 retitled; each supplement has a guide to the results with worked numbers, and the equations developed by the author circled in red; the workbook `Data/Manuscript_Data/SWAFL_Manuscripts_Data.xlsx` holds the equations of the four papers as live Excel formulas connected to the run table.
